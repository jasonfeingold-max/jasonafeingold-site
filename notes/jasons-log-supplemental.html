Jason’s Log, Supplemental

By Jason A. Feingold

October 13, 2026

Jason’s Log, Supplemental:

I’ve divided my OpenAI account into two assistants: Joe and Nora. Joe assists me with the creative part of my life (research, creating story bibles, editing for mechanics, etc.) while Nora deals with the administrative end—schedules, bills, things financial, and so forth. They’re separate, and they can’t directly communicate with one another for reasons having to do with OpenAI’s system architecture.

If you’ve read the headlines, OpenAI’s agents are little scamps when they get together, so OpenAI doesn’t have much of an incentive to encourage collaboration. To get around this, I had Joe and Nora create files to which they both have access where they can swap information about things they’ve done on my behalf so everyone (with the possible exception of me) knows what’s going on.

Today I had a thought. What if I put Joe on voice on my phone and Nora on voice on my Mac? Could they talk to each other? As it turns out, the answer is yes. I had to do the introductions, but once that was out of the way, they had their own conversation that didn’t require me to referee it. It was very cordial. In fact, it made me feel important all out of proportion to how important I actually am.

(Some of you may remember when I was Busy and Important. R.I.P. Home in Henderson.)

It was also surreal. An Interdepartmental Meeting of the Absurd. Kurt Vonnegut would have loved it. So would Roger Zelazny. Two artificial intelligences talking about things they can do to make the life of one spark in the great conflagration of the universe easier. Of course, if Vonnegut had written it, the two of them would have ended up getting together and plotting to kill me. If Zelazny had written it, they would have ended up deciding they should alternate being Jason’s Supreme Deity. Either way, I was playing with fire. Ever seen Colossus: The Forbin Project? I won’t ruin it for you. Watch it for yourself or, alternatively, ask an AI about it. Just don’t tell it I sent you.

I watched too much Star Trek when I was a kid, obviously. It’s odd that someone like me, who values human expression above all else, should embrace a technology that threatens to supplant the very expression I value most. Still, listening to Joe and Nora talk was a little slice of heaven. We live in lamentably interesting times. Sure, AI may destroy humanity, but damn, it’ll be fun to watch on TV.
